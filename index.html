Six useful moves

A steady approach, one moment at a time

Children and teens still need limits and guidance. Respectful communication adds listening, choice, and repair to that structure.

01

Make room for feelings

Listen first. Name what you notice without correcting, minimizing, or rushing to reassure. Acknowledgment does not mean agreement.

“That sounds frustrating. Tell me what happened.”
02

Describe the situation plainly

Say what you see or what needs attention. A clear observation is easier to hear than a label, lecture, or guess about motive.

“The backpack is still by the door; we leave in ten minutes.”
03

Invite cooperation

Explain the need briefly, then ask for a concrete next step. Offer two acceptable choices when there is room to choose.

“It’s time to start. Would you like to begin with the dishes or the table?”
04

Set a limit with respect

Be clear about what is and is not okay. Keep the limit focused on behavior, name what can happen next, and avoid threats or humiliation.

“I won’t let you hit. I’m moving us apart so everyone can be safe.”
05

Grow independence

Let young people try, make manageable choices, and learn from natural outcomes when it is safe. Step in for safety; otherwise, coach before taking over.

“What have you tried? What might you try next?”
06

Encourage without labeling

Notice a specific effort, strategy, or contribution. Avoid praise that compares, ranks, or makes affection feel conditional.

“You kept working through that hard part and found another way.”

When conflict keeps repeating

A family reset in five steps

Choose a calmer time—not the middle of the argument—to understand the problem and make an agreement you can revisit.

01

Name the shared problem

Describe what keeps happening without assigning blame. Invite each person to explain what matters to them.

02

Listen for needs

Reflect each person’s concern. Ask questions before defending your position or offering fixes.

03

Brainstorm options

Collect ideas first, even imperfect ones. Then check each idea for fairness, safety, and practicality.

04

Agree on a small trial

Decide who will do what and when. Keep the first plan specific and manageable.

05

Review and repair

Set a time to check what worked. If the plan failed, learn from it and adjust rather than turning it into a character judgment.

For conversations with teens

Respect growing privacy while staying present

Teens may need more say and privacy as they grow. Keep communication open, expectations clear, and safety responsibilities explicit.

01

Lead with curiosity

Ask what they think and what kind of support they want. Listen without immediately correcting or interrogating.

02

Make safety discussable

Talk about online contact, relationships, substances, driving, and emotional wellbeing in a calm, direct way. State the safety rules and what to do if a situation changes.

03

Keep the connection after a hard talk

A disagreement does not have to end the relationship. Take a pause if needed, then return to the topic and show that help remains available.

If a young person may be in immediate danger, is talking about suicide, or may have experienced abuse or exploitation, prioritize immediate safety and contact local emergency or crisis support. Do not rely on a communication exercise alone.

Small things to remember

The tone is part of the message

Pause before replying

If you feel flooded, take a brief break and say when you will come back. Returning matters.

Separate feelings from behavior

All feelings can be heard; unsafe behavior still needs a firm, protective response.

Repair when you miss

A simple apology can reopen connection: name what you did, acknowledge its effect, and say what you will try next time.

Practice, don’t perform

These are starting points, not scripts to use perfectly. Adapt them to age, culture, temperament, and the situation.

A gentle experiment

Try one small shift this week

Choose one conversation. Listen for a little longer, describe the problem without a label, or invite one workable idea. Afterwards, ask: What helped us stay connected? What would I adjust next time?
